In April, I transplanted my tomato plants into the garden. They were all looking strong, but the first week of May, I noticed some leaves starting to curl and turn yellow. I suspected early blight and sprayed the plants with a mixture of neem oil and water. By the end of the month, a few plants had already bolted, meaning they were going to flower and fruit prematurely, which I knew would lower the yield. I picked the flowers off those plants to prevent seed formation.

June brought the real heat, and my tomatoes really struggled. I started hand-watering them every other day, which seemed to help keep them hydrated. The biggest issue in June was the tomato hornworms. I noticed a couple of these pesky critters eating holes in the foliage, which had to be removed by hand. Afterward, I sprayed them with BT (Bacillus thuringiensis) to deter them.

July was a bit better. The plants were producing decent fruit, but I had to keep an eye out for the tomato fruitworms. I saw some damage on the developing fruit, so I picked off those fruits and sprayed the plants with BT again. The tomatoes seemed to recover from the earlier stress of bolting and the heat.

August was the month of the biggest harvest. I had about 20 tomatoes in each plant, which was great considering how much heat they had gone through. However, towards the end of August, I noticed some brown spots on the bottom leaves of a few plants. I suspected the plants might be suffering from early blight again, so I started misting the soil to increase moisture retention and sprayed the leaves with a copper-based fungicide.

September was all about harvesting. I picked the fruits almost every other day, and the plants seemed to be in good condition. There was a lot of foliage, and the tomatoes were plump and bright red. I harvested all the tomatoes before the first frost in mid-October.

The season taught me to be more proactive in pest management and water retention, especially during the hottest months. I learned that sometimes the best way to deal with stress on plants is to give them the right conditions - plenty of water and a bit of shade.
