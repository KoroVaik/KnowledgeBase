I've been dabbling in fermentation for a while now, and I've found that making sauerkraut and kimchi at home is a lot of fun, but also a bit of a challenge. The key seems to be getting the salt ratio just right. I've learned that too much salt can cause the vegetables to shrivel up and turn brown, while too little can lead to an overly sour and bitter taste. I've used ratios like 1 tablespoon of salt per 10 cups of cabbage, and it's worked out well for me so far.

Another issue I've come across is mold. I've found that keeping the jars tightly sealed and refrigerating them after the initial 2-3 days of fermentation can help prevent mold from growing. If mold does start to appear, it's best to throw the batch away. The good news is that you can usually prevent mold with careful storage and monitoring.

The best part of making these fermented foods is the flavor and texture they add to my meals. I'll often use sauerkraut in sandwiches or on top of salads, and kimchi is a staple in my Korean-inspired dishes. It's a bit of an adventure every time you make them, but the results are definitely worth the effort. Plus, they're super healthy and packed with probiotics, which is always a plus in my book.
