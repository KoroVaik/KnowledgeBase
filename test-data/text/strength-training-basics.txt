Starting strength training can be a game-changer for physical fitness and overall health. The basics include the squat, deadlift, and bench press. Each has its own unique form and benefits, which I've been focusing on recently. Here’s what I’ve learned and a simple progression I've been following:

First up is the squat. The goal is to lower your body until your hips are at least parallel to the ground, keeping your back straight and core engaged. The bar should be placed on the middle of your traps (shoulder blades) and the movement should be slow and controlled. It's all about maintaining proper form, especially focusing on keeping your chest up, elbows out, and feet flat on the ground.

Next is the deadlift. This one is all about lifting from the floor to standing up, keeping your back straight and your head up. The bar should be placed on the middle of your traps and then lifted up slowly, ensuring your knees are bent and you're using your legs to stand up, not your back. The key is to maintain a neutral spine and avoid rounding your back.

Lastly, the bench press. Set up with a barbell on the rack and your feet shoulder-width apart. Lie down on the bench, keeping your back straight. Use the arms to lift the barbell off the rack, keeping your elbows tucked in and your chest up. Lower the barbell to your chest and then push it back up to starting position. Focus on the lift and lower phases, ensuring you're not using momentum.

A simple progression I'm following includes:

- Week 1: 3 sets of 8 reps of each exercise, rest 1 minute between sets.
- Week 2: 4 sets of 8 reps, rest 1 minute between sets.
- Week 3: 4 sets of 10 reps, rest 1 minute between sets.
- Week 4: 5 sets of 10 reps, rest 1 minute between sets.
- Week 5: 5 sets of 12 reps, rest 1 minute between sets.

The goal is to gradually increase the number of reps and sets to build strength and endurance. Remember, form is everything. It’s okay to take as long as needed to ensure you’re doing the exercises correctly. Safety always comes first. Over time, you'll see and feel the benefits, and your fitness journey will be much more enjoyable.
