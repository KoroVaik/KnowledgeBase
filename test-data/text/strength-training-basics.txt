I've always been a bit of a couch potato, you know? Sitting around watching TV and eating too much junk food. But then, about six months ago, I decided it was time to make a change. I wanted to be in better shape, look better, and just generally feel more confident in my daily life. So, I started doing some research on how to get started with strength training and what exercises I should be focusing on.

After some reading and a bit of trial and error, I decided to focus on three basic exercises: squats, deadlifts, and bench presses. These three movements are the foundation of any strength training program and they will work almost every muscle in your body. Plus, they are a great way to build a solid base of strength and improve your overall fitness level.

When it comes to form, it's important to remember that proper technique is key. You don't want to do these exercises just to get it over with or because you're in a rush. You want to do them correctly so you can get the most out of them and reduce the risk of injury.

For squats, start by standing with your feet shoulder-width apart and your hands gripping a barbell or dumbbells. Keep your chest up and your core engaged as you bend your knees and lower your body down towards the ground. Push back up and repeat. Make sure to keep your back straight and avoid letting your knees collapse inwards. It's also important to keep your weight balanced over your heels and toes, shifting your weight slightly forward as you squat and then shifting back when you stand.

Deadlifts are a great exercise for building strength in the lower body and for improving overall core strength. To do them, stand with your feet hip-width apart and bend over to pick up a barbell or dumbbells off the ground. Keep your back straight and drive your hips back as you stand up, keeping your back straight. As you come up, the movement should feel like you are pushing yourself up with your legs rather than pulling the weight up with your back. It's important to keep your core engaged and keep your chin tucked in to protect your neck.

Bench presses are a great exercise for building upper body strength and for improving chest size and strength. To do them, lie down on a bench with your feet flat on the ground and your arms extended above your chest, gripping the barbell or dumbbells. Lower the bar down towards your chest and then push it back up, keeping your elbows tucked in. It's important to keep your core engaged and to avoid flaring your chest out as you press the weight up.

Once you've got the basics down, it's time to start building a simple progression. Start by doing three sets of ten reps for each exercise, resting for 90 seconds between sets. As you get stronger and more comfortable with the exercises, you can gradually increase the number of reps or the weight you're using. For example, you could do four sets of five reps, or five sets of four reps. As you increase the number of reps or the weight, make sure to maintain proper form and avoid sacrificing technique for more reps or weight.

It's also important to mix up your routine every few weeks. You don't want to do the same three exercises every single day or you'll hit a plateau. Try adding in some other exercises, like rows or squats, to keep things interesting and to work different muscle groups. You could also try doing a split routine, where you focus on a specific muscle group on one day and then another on the next.

Finally, make sure to listen to your body and take breaks when you need them. Strength training can be tough and it's important to give your muscles time to recover. If you're feeling sore or achy after a workout, take a day or two off to let your muscles rest. And if you're feeling really exhausted, it's okay to take a break from weightlifting for a few days or even a week.

Overall, I've found that starting with squats, deadlifts, and bench presses has been a great way to get started with strength training. These three exercises are a great place to start and they will help you build a solid base of strength and improve your overall fitness level. As you get stronger and more comfortable with the exercises, you can gradually increase the number of reps or the weight you're using, and mix up your routine every few weeks to keep things interesting. Just remember to listen to your body and take breaks when you need them, and you'll see results in no time!
