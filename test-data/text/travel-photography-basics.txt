Hey there, me again. So, you're diving into travel photography and finding it a bit tricky with just your phone camera? Well, I've got some tips to make your shots more captivating, even without fancy equipment. Let’s talk about composition, light, and editing basics.

First up: Composition. Think of your frame as a canvas. Use the rule of thirds, where you imagine dividing your screen into thirds both horizontally and vertically. Place your main subject along one of those lines or at the intersection points for a more dynamic shot. Also, try leading lines - think roads, rivers, or even fence lines - that guide the viewer’s eye through your photo.

Lighting is your friend. Catch the golden hour, that soft light just before sunrise or just after sunset, and it’s like nature’s magic lighting. Try to avoid harsh midday sun as it can create sharp shadows and overly bright spots. If you’re in the shade, use the ambient light. Also, a bit of natural light can add interest and warmth to your photos.

Editing is the cherry on top. Keep it simple with apps like Lightroom or VSCO. Focus on a few basic things: adjust your exposure and contrast to bring out the best in your image. Don’t overdo the saturation; a bit of pop can really make colors pop. Use the healing tool to remove any imperfections in the background and sharpen your photo a little. And always remember, less is more. Don’t clutter your photo with too much editing. Less is often better when it comes to making your photo tell a story.

Practice these techniques over time, and you’ll see your travel photos really start to shine! Hope this helps!
