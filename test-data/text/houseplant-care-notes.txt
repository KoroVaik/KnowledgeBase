### Houseplant Care Log

#### Introduction
Hey there, it’s me again! Here’s a quick dive into my houseplant care routine that I’ve been keeping track of in my own little digital notebook. This log is more than just a simple record; it’s a testament to my evolving relationship with my plants and a reminder of my dedication to nurturing them. I hope sharing this with you might inspire you to take a closer look at your own green companions and the care they need to thrive.

#### Watering Schedules and General Care
- **Monday:**
  - **Plants Watered:** Peace Lily, Snake Plant, and Aloe Vera
  - **Watering Frequency:** Peace Lily - 1 time per week, Snake Plant - every 2 weeks, Aloe Vera - every 3 weeks
  - **Notes:** Peace Lily thrives on the moist soil, so I usually check for dryness around the base and give it a good soak. Snake Plant doesn’t mind if it gets a bit dry between waterings, and I try to ensure the soil is just damp to prevent root rot. Aloe Vera is the most forgiving, so I only check the soil around the base of the plant, and if it feels dry, I give it a gentle drink.

- **Tuesday:**
  - **Plants Watered:** Fiddle Leaf Fig
  - **Watering Frequency:** Every 2 weeks
  - **Notes:** Fiddle Leaf Fig needs a bit more attention. The soil should be dry to the touch before I water. I use a watering can with a narrow spout to avoid getting water on the leaves, as it can cause spotting and yellowing.

- **Wednesday:**
  - **Plants Watered:** Spider Plant
  - **Watering Frequency:** Every 3 weeks
  - **Notes:** Spider Plant is a very forgiving plant when it comes to watering. If the soil feels dry, it’s time to water. I usually give it a good drink to moisten the soil throughout the pot.

- **Thursday:**
  - **Plants Watered:** ZZ Plant
  - **Watering Frequency:** Every 4 weeks
  - **Notes:** ZZ Plant is a slow grower, and it’s perfect for my busy schedule. If the soil is dry to the touch, it’s time to water. ZZ Plant thrives on soil that is just damp, and I usually avoid overwatering to prevent root rot.

- **Friday:**
  - **Plants Watered:** Pothos
  - **Watering Frequency:** Every 3 weeks
  - **Notes:** Pothos is another forgiving plant. I check the soil around the base and give it a drink if it’s dry. Pothos loves humidity, so I sometimes mist its leaves if the air in my apartment is a bit dry.

- **Saturday:**
  - **Plants Watered:** Monstera
  - **Watering Frequency:** Every 3 weeks
  - **Notes:** Monstera is a bit pickier when it comes to watering. I usually check the soil around the base and give it a drink if it’s dry. Monstera thrives on slightly moist soil and can handle occasional dry spells, but it’s always best to err on the side of damp soil.

- **Sunday:**
  - **Plants Watered:** Dracaena
  - **Watering Frequency:** Every 2 weeks
  - **Notes:** Dracaena is another plant that can handle a bit of dryness. I usually check the soil around the base and give it a drink if it’s dry. Dracaena thrives on slightly moist soil and can tolerate dry conditions if the soil is not overly wet.

#### Specific Plants and Their Challenges
There’s a few plants that just keep giving me a run for my money. I’ve learned a lot from them too, so let’s talk about each one individually.

- **Peace Lily (Spathiphyllum):**
  - **Problems:** Yellow leaves, leaf drop
  - **Possible Causes:**
    - Overwatering
    - Underwatering
    - Lack of light
    - Low humidity
  - **Solution:** I’ve found that overwatering is the main culprit. I’ve started to check the soil more often and only give it a drink when the top inch is dry. I also ensure it gets enough light and humidity to prevent yellowing of the leaves. If it starts to drop leaves, I make sure it’s getting enough nutrients by feeding it every 2 weeks with a balanced fertilizer.
