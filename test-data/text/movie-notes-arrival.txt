In my journal, I've been reflecting on the movie "Arrival" and how it explores the profound intersection of language and time. Watching it again, I was struck by how the film not only showcases the complexity of language but also delves into the nature of time and how both elements can be used to understand and connect with other beings.

The main character, Dr. Louise Banks, played by Amy Adams, is tasked with helping an alien species that has arrived on Earth. The aliens communicate through what appears to be a visual language, which is a fascinating concept. The film highlights the fluidity and adaptability of language, as Dr. Banks and her team learn to interpret the alien messages and develop a vocabulary that transcends their human limitations.

But what really captivated me was the idea of time. The aliens are able to manipulate and experience time in a way that seems foreign to humans. This raises the question: can language be a tool to alter or understand the passage of time? The film suggests that it can, and that through their shared language, humans and the aliens can begin to bridge the gap in their understanding of time.

The final scenes, where the characters come together to watch a human child play, are a poignant reminder of how language and time can be used to connect with each other. It's a thought-provoking idea that resonates with the human condition, where our ability to communicate and perceive time shapes our experiences and relationships.

Overall, "Arrival" is a profound exploration of how language and time intersect to create a deeper understanding of the universe and ourselves. It's a movie that makes you think about the power of communication and how it can shape our perception of the world.
