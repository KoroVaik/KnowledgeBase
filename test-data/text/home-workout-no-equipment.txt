Since I can’t hit the gym, this workout has become a staple in my weekly fitness plan. It’s amazing how much you can achieve with just your bodyweight and a bit of space in your home. Here’s what I’ve been doing and how it’s been going for me.

### The Routine: Monday, Wednesday, Friday

#### Day 1: Full-Body Workout

##### Warm-Up (5-10 Minutes)

- **Marching Jumps**: Stand with your feet shoulder-width apart. Jump up and down, keeping your knees slightly bent, for 30 seconds. This gets your heart rate up and your muscles ready for the session.
- **Arm Circles**: Stand with your feet shoulder-width apart. Bring your arms up to shoulder level and make a big circle with both arms for 20 seconds in each direction. This helps loosen up the shoulders and neck.
- **High Knees**: Run in place, bringing your knees up to your chest, for 30 seconds. This is a great cardio move and also works your legs.

##### Core and Upper Body (30 Minutes)

- **Plank**: Start with your hands and feet shoulder-width apart in a plank position. Hold for 30 seconds to 1 minute. This can be split into multiple 30-second holds with 10-second rest in between if needed.
- **Mountain Climbers**: From the plank position, bring one knee up to your chest, then the other. Alternate as quickly as you can for 30 seconds. This really hits your core and lower body.
- **Push-ups**: Start with a regular push-up if you can, or a knee push-up if it's too difficult. Do as many as you can, aiming for at least 15. This is a great way to work your upper body.
- **One-Arm Rows**: Start with your right hand extended in front of you. Bring your left arm back and down until your elbow is almost touching your ribcage. Switch arms and repeat. Aim for 10 reps on each side.
- **Dumbbell Swings**: If you have dumbbells, use them for this. Hold the weights in front of your chest, then swing them up and down, keeping your arms bent and your core engaged. Do 15 reps.

##### Cool Down and Stretch (5-10 Minutes)

- **Deep Breathing**: Take deep breaths, inhaling for 5 seconds and exhaling for 5 seconds. This helps calm your mind and body after the intense workout.
- **Static Stretches**: Spend a few minutes doing stretches for your arms, legs, and back. Make sure you hold each stretch for 20-30 seconds.

#### Day 2: Leg and Butt Focus

##### Warm-Up (5-10 Minutes)

- **Jumping Jacks**: Get your heart rate up with a minute of jumping jacks.
- **Leg Swings**: Stand with your feet together and swing your legs from side to side, then front to back. This loosens up your hips and legs.
- **Butterfly Stretches**: Sit with your knees bent and feet together. Open your knees wide and hold this for 30 seconds. This helps stretch your hips.

##### Leg and Butt Exercises (30 Minutes)

- **Squats**: Start with a regular squat if you can, or a knee squat if it's easier. Aim for 20 reps. This is a great all-around leg exercise.
- **Lunges**: Stand with your feet together, take a big step forward with your right foot, and bend both knees until your front knee is almost at a 90-degree angle. Hold for a second, then step back to the start position. Switch legs and repeat. Aim for 15 reps on each side.
- **Leg Raises**: Lie on your back with your knees bent and feet flat on the floor. Lift one leg up at a time, keeping your foot in a pointed position. Hold each leg lift for 2 seconds, then lower back to the floor. Aim for 10 reps on each leg.
- **Bicycle Crunches**: Lie on your back with your knees bent and feet flat on the floor. Bring your left knee in towards your chest while extending your right leg out to the side. Bring your right knee in towards your chest while extending your left leg out to the side. Continue alternating this movement for 30 seconds. This is a great core and oblique exercise.

##### Cool Down and Stretch (5-10 Minutes)

- **Deep Breathing**: As with the first workout, take deep breaths to calm down.
- **Static Stretches**: Spend time stretching your legs, hip flexors, and lower back.

#### Day 3: Rest or Light Activity

This day is for recovery and rest. Depending on how your body feels, you can do a light walk or some light stretching, or take a day off entirely. Sometimes a day of rest can be more beneficial than another workout.

### The Benefits

After a few weeks of this routine, I’ve noticed a significant improvement in my strength and endurance. The best part is that I can easily adjust the intensity and volume of the exercises based on how my body feels that day. If I’m feeling extra tired, I might do fewer reps or take a rest day. If I’m feeling energetic, I can push myself harder.

### Tips and Tricks

- **Progress Tracking**: I’ve been keeping a log of my workouts, noting how many reps I did, how long I held the plank, etc. This helps me see progress and also gives me a reason to aim for better results the next day.
- **Consistency is Key**: While it’s tempting to skip days, the real benefits come from consistency. I try to stick to the routine as much as possible, and the results speak for themselves.
- **Stay Hydrated**: It’s crucial to stay hydrated, especially when you’re doing intense workouts. Make sure you drink plenty of water before and after your workout.
- **Listen to Your Body**: If an exercise feels too tough or you’re injured, don’t force it. Modify the exercise or take a rest day. Your body is your temple, and you need to respect it.
