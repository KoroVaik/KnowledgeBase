Hey, remember the first time I picked up a kitchen knife? It was a bit scary, but I knew I needed to learn how to chop vegetables safely. So, I started practicing with my grandma's knife, which is super sharp but not exactly beginner-friendly. I practiced my grip first—using a three-finger grip where the knife is held between my thumb, index, and middle fingers. That way, I can control the knife's movement and avoid any accidents.

Then, I moved on to julienne, which is basically cutting vegetables into long, thin sticks. I found it easiest to use a mandoline slicer for this, which helps get uniform cuts every time. But practice made perfect, and soon enough, I was cutting veggies for my mom's stir-fry just like a pro.

Next up was dicing an onion. This is a bit more challenging, but so rewarding! I learned to hold the onion with my non-dominant hand and cut off the tip. Then, I used a cross-hatch method: making small cuts all the way down and across the onion. This way, you can easily dice it into small, even pieces. It's important to remember to keep your fingers tucked away from the knife and to always cut away from your body, never towards it. 

Now, I feel like I'm a kitchen knife pro! Practice makes perfect, and once you get the hang of these basic skills, it'll be a breeze to chop up any veggies you need for your next meal. Safe cutting always!
