The first step is learning how to hold a knife correctly. There are a few grips you can learn, but the one that's most common and comfortable for most people is the "Finger-Hugger" grip. This is where you wrap your fingers around the handle of the knife, keeping your thumb and index finger on the blade. Your hand should be slightly cupped around the handle, and your thumb should be on the back of the blade, with the knife positioned to your right. This grip allows you to have good control over the knife and helps you maintain a steady hand.

Next up, let's talk about julienne. Julienne is a cooking technique where you cut ingredients, usually vegetables or meats, into long, thin strips of equal thickness and width. This is a very common technique in cooking and is used to add a lot of visual appeal to dishes. To julienne an onion, for example, you'll need to have it peeled and halved. Then, cut it lengthwise into thin slices, about 1/8 of an inch wide. Make sure to cut against the grain to get long, thin strips. Practice makes perfect, and you'll be able to julienne in no time.

Moving on to dicing an onion, this is another common cutting technique. To do this, you'll need to have your onion peeled and halved. Place the onion flat side down on your cutting board. Using the knife, cut the onion into squares of about 1/4 inch. Then, cut each square diagonally in half to make triangles. Finally, cut each triangle in half to make smaller triangles, then cut each triangle in half again to make small cubes. This is the basis for dicing, and once you've got the hang of it, you can use it to make any diced ingredients you need.

Safety is the most important thing when it comes to kitchen knife skills. Always make sure you have a stable cutting board and a clean knife. When you're not cutting, always keep your knife in a safe place, and never leave it out where kids or pets can get to it.
